🔒 Sound Share Privacy Policy

Plain words • 1-minute read

1. What my app saves, and why

  • Your email and scrambled password (or your GitHub account info): So you can sign up, sign in, and keep your account yours. If you sign in with GitHub, we get your basic account info from GitHub instead of a password. If you use email, we never see your real password—only a scrambled code.
  • Your username: So other people know who shared a clip with them without seeing your email.
  • Your audio files, titles, and sharing settings: Saved in our storage and database so you can store, play, and share your sounds.
  • Reports and blocked users: When you report a clip/user or block someone, we save that record so staff can review rule-breaking content and so blocked users' clips stay hidden from you.
  • Temporary device permission (not saved): When you click Enable Output Device Detection, the browser asks for microphone access just so it can list your speakers and virtual cables. We immediately stop the connection and never save or record anything from it.

2. Who can see it

  • Your email: Only you (and me, the app creator, in the database).
  • Private clips: Only you in the app (though as the app creator, I can technically see stored files inside the Supabase database).
  • Shared clips: Only you and the exact usernames you list.
  • Public clips: Everyone signed in to the app, plus moderators, admins, and owners who keep the public feed safe.
  • Reports: Only you when submitting, and moderators, admins, and owners in the Staff Panel.

3. How to delete it

  • One clip at a time: Click the Delete button next to any clip in your Personal Vault. This removes both the sound file and its title.
  • Delete My Data & Account button (in Settings): Permanently removes your login account, username, profile, and all of your saved audio files. It does not remove copies someone else already downloaded to their own phone or computer.

4. What my app never saves

We never save your exact location or GPS data, your real plain-text password, your GitHub password, your contacts or phone address book, or any live audio from your microphone.
